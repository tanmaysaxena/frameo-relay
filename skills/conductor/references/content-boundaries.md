# Content boundaries for romantic or heated scenes

Rule for the whole relay (injector, scene-prep, conductor, review). Learned on an early romance job.

## The ceiling is the script's, not the filter's
- Every script states, or the injector asks for, a **sensuality ceiling** per scene (for example "mainstream B-grade cinema: silhouettes, steam, shadows only"). Write it into `style.avoid` and into the checklist row. Prompts never go above it.
- Default when the script is silent: clothed, framed above the shoulders, kiss and embrace at most; intimacy implied by shadow, steam, a closing door, lighting.
- A script line that asks for more than that is not obeyed silently. The injector flags it in the plan and the owner picks the ceiling in words (clothed / implied / silhouette). Nothing explicit is generated.

## Ceiling tiers the owner can pick (in words, per scene or chapter)
1. **clothed**: framed above the shoulders, kiss and embrace at most.
2. **implied / silhouette**: shadows, steam, frosted glass, a closing door; nobody undressed on screen.
3. **mainstream-drama nudity, nothing explicit** (the level of Game of Thrones and similar prestige series): adult characters only, intimacy shown the way those shows show it. Bare breasts, back and buttocks are allowed in love scenes and in non-sexual moments (bath, dressing, waking), lit and framed as drama, not as a display. Never shown: penetration or any explicit sex act, visible genitals, close-ups that dwell on the body, fetish framing, anyone who is or looks under 18. Camera stays on faces, shoulders, torsos and the whole-body wide, then cuts away, as the shows do; intimacy resolves by cut, shadow, steam or a door. The shot is written as composition and action (embrace, her back to camera, sheet at the waist), the heat in the faces and the breath. Heated segments are still keyframed first; a refused clip still gets ONE pre-written weaker fallback, never a rephrase, and never a ladder up to the filter.
- Tier 3 is chosen by the owner for named scenes or chapters; never default to it. Without that choice the ceiling is the script's lower tier.

## Never probe the moderation filter
- No "start strong and step down until it passes" ladders, no re-wording a refused prompt to sneak past the same filter, no test clips to find where the cut-off is. That is evasion, not direction, and it burns credits.
- A refused clip gets ONE planned fallback, written in advance by scene-prep and weaker in content, not rephrased: for a kiss scene, foreheads touching, steam, a door closing, a cut to shadow. A refund does not use the retake allowance. If the fallback is refused too, report it plainly and stop that shot.

## How to keep heat without crossing it
- Use implication the way film does: cast shadows (a shirt as a shadow on a doorknob), two silhouettes merging on a wall, frosted glass, steam curling up a doorframe, a lamp dimming, motion lines, onomatopoeia (thump, fwip, SMOOCH) dissolving into halftone dots, a closing door.
- Camera discipline written into the prompt: "stays above the shoulders", "plays entirely on the door and wall".

## Roles
- `scene-prep`: write the ceiling into the sealed prompt and pre-write the softer fallback for any heated segment; do the moderation-risk look at the keyframe stage (generate those keyframes first).
- `review`: reject a frame that goes beyond the ceiling, even if the model produced it; cite the frame.
- `conductor`: never raise the ceiling to retry; never spend allowance on rewording.
